Part 6 · Research · market · the gap

Most sign-language apps are not built for us

Open competitive analysis PDF

The best-known apps are English-based, and the Arabic apps on the stores cover only one piece of the problem.

What is on the stores today

  • Hand Talk, Lingvano, PopSign, Sign Language AI — English-based: ASL, BSL, Libras. No Arabic, no Egyptian Sign Language.
  • Al-Turjuman — Arabic, but one direction only: speech/text → sign. It cannot read a Deaf person’s signs.
  • Arabic SL App for the Deaf — a static learning library: no AI, no translation.
  • BrightSign — needs an expensive smart glove. Ava — captions only, no sign language.
  • No app combines translation, learning, gamification, kids and community.

What Eshara brings

  • Arabic first, starting with Egyptian Sign Language and Egyptian Arabic voice.
  • Two-way live translation with the phone camera only — sign → text → speech and voice → text → sign.
  • A 3D avatar tied to translation, dictionary and lessons.
  • Learning with camera practice, plus streak, XP, daily challenges and a leaderboard.
  • A safe Kids mode, chat and calls with live captions, and an offline emergency screen.
