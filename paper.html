Eshara: Bidirectional Arabic Sign Language Communication with Landmark-Based Recognition on Web and Mobile

Eshara team · Supervised by Dr. Ahmed Hamza · Teaching assistant Eng. Basant Mohamed

Abstract

Deaf and hard-of-hearing Arabic speakers still lack everyday tools that translate between Arabic sign language and spoken or written Arabic. Eshara is a web and mobile platform that translates in both directions: sign to text through the camera, and text or voice to sign through an animated avatar, with a dictionary, gamified learning, chat and an emergency mode in its design.

Recognition runs on hand landmarks rather than raw images. MediaPipe extracts 21 points per hand, and a compact multilayer perceptron classifies the resulting 126-value vector into 31 Arabic letters.

Results so far

Trained on the public ArASL images combined with 30,400 landmark samples the team recorded with a purpose-built collection tool, the letter model reaches 93.3% accuracy on a held-out test set and trains in 10 to 15 minutes on a CPU. The Flutter app already uses it for live, letter-by-letter sign-to-text translation, and the web app delivers the full interface with mocked services.

Problem, approach and contributions

01

Problem

  • Communication between deaf and hearing people depends on interpreters who are rarely available
  • Arabic sign language tools are few, and most published systems are research prototypes, not daily apps
  • Arabic datasets come from few signers and mostly from Saudi or unified sign language
02

Approach

  • Landmarks instead of pixels, so recognition stays light enough for real-time use
  • One shared AI service used by a Flutter app and a Next.js web app
  • Our own data collection tool to grow the dataset beyond public images
03

Contributions

  • A working sign-to-text pipeline from camera to letters, connected to a trained model
  • A team dataset of 30,400 landmark samples across 31 letters
  • A complete UX specification: 10 user-flow diagrams, Kids Mode and emergency flows
